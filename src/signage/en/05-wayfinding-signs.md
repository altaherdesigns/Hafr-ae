Wayfinding signs get people from the gate to the right door without asking anyone. We make them for buildings, schools, clinics, warehouses and residential compounds, in Arabic and English, cut and finished in our Fujairah workshop.

## The sign family

A wayfinding system is a small set of sign types repeated through the building.

1. **Site and entrance signs** name the building at the gate. See [pylon and monument signs](/signage/pylon-monument-signs/).
2. **Directories** at the entrance and each lift lobby list what is on each floor.
3. **Directional signs** at every decision point, where a corridor splits or a path turns.
4. **Identification signs** at each door: room names and numbers.
5. **Parking and site signs** for entries, exits, bays and drop-off points.

## Start with a sign schedule

Before anything is made, we list every sign in a schedule: its type, its text in both languages, where it goes and its size. You check the schedule against the building, and it becomes the order. This avoids the most common problem in wayfinding: a missing sign at a turn nobody walked through on paper.

## Readable from where people stand

| Sign | Typical letter height | Typical mounting height |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Room and door signs | 15 to 25 mm | About 1.5 m to the centre of the sign |
| Corridor directional signs | 25 to 50 mm | Wall-mounted at eye level, or overhead above head height |
| Lift lobby directories | 15 to 25 mm for names, larger for floor numbers | About 1.5 m to the centre |
| External site signs | 75 mm and up | Set for reading from a car |

Letter height should grow with reading distance. Our [house number size guide](/blog/house-number-size-visibility/) explains the rule of thumb.

## Materials

Indoors, brushed aluminium, stainless steel and acrylic are common, often with cut or engraved text. Outdoors, choose powder-coated aluminium or stainless steel posts and panels, with fixings that will not rust near the sea.

## Arabic first, English second

Most UAE buildings put Arabic on the right or on top, with English below or to the left. Arrows must point the same way in both. We set both languages together and a second Arabic reader checks every line before cutting.
