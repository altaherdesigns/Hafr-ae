A house number has one job: to be found. A delivery driver, a taxi, a guest arriving for the first time and, in an emergency, a paramedic all need to read your number quickly and correctly. Yet many villa numbers are small, low contrast or hidden behind a plant.

This guide covers the practical choices that make a number readable: size, font, contrast, position and lighting.

## How big should the digits be?

A simple rule of thumb for signs is about 25 mm of digit height for every 3 m of viewing distance, in good light and face on. That gives:

| Digit height | Readable at roughly |
|---|---|
| 75 mm | 9 m |
| 100 mm | 12 m |
| 150 mm | 18 m |
| 200 mm | 24 m |

This is a guideline for comfortable reading, not an exact limit. Reduce the distance if the light is poor, the viewer is in a moving car, the number is seen at an angle, or the contrast is weak. If your gate is set back from the road, or the street is wide, go bigger.

## Think about the real viewing distance

Measure from the place where someone first needs to read the number, which is usually the road or the car park entrance, not the gate. Many villa communities have wide streets with cars parked on both sides. A driver looking for a number at walking speed needs more size than a pedestrian at the gate.

## Font: keep it simple

For numbers, simple wins. Choose a clean sans serif or a clear geometric style with:

- Uniform stroke thickness
- Open shapes in 3, 5, 6, 8 and 9 that cannot be confused
- Generous spacing between digits
- No script or decorative flourishes

Avoid thin hairline fonts. Thin strokes disappear at distance and are fragile when cut in metal. A medium or bold weight stays readable.

If you want Arabic-Indic numerals (١٢٣) for style, consider showing them smaller, with the standard digits (123) large. That is a suggestion rather than a rule: it keeps the number easy to read for navigation apps, delivery drivers and visitors who read Latin digits first. Ask your community what is expected.

## Contrast is more important than colour

Readability comes from the difference between the digits and the surface behind them. Some pairings that work well:

- Brushed stainless digits on a dark painted wall
- Matte black digits on a light render
- White powder-coated digits on dark stone

Pairings that struggle: stainless on pale stone, black on dark grey, and any colour that matches the wall closely. Glossy surfaces create glare in strong sun and make the digits harder to read from certain angles. Matte or brushed finishes are easier on the eye.

## Position: where people will look

Put the number where it faces the approach, not just where it is convenient to fix. Ask yourself:

- Is it visible from the street, or only from the gate?
- Could a palm, a hedge, a parked car or an open gate block it?
- Is it at a sensible height, not too low or too high?
- Is it on a fixed surface, not a gate leaf that will be open when a visitor arrives?

A number on the pier beside the gate is usually best, because the pier does not move. For a more complete look at the whole entrance, see [planning a villa entrance](/blog/villa-entrance-design-sign-gate-lighting/).

## Making numbers readable at night

After dark, an unlit number depends on street lights and car headlights. Options:

- **Front lighting:** a small wall light shines on the digits.
- **Halo lighting:** LEDs behind the digits glow around them, so the number reads as a bright outline.
- **Face-lit digits:** the digits themselves light up.

If you are choosing, [backlit vs non-backlit](/blog/backlit-vs-non-backlit-villa-sign/) walks through the details. For a number, a soft halo is often enough, because the digits are bold and large. Keep brightness modest to avoid glare.

## Cut metal numbers

Cut or fabricated metal digits last well outdoors. Choose the material to match the environment: 316 stainless near the sea, or powder-coated aluminium and steel for colour. Our [material guide](/blog/best-material-villa-name-plate-uae/) explains the differences. A few cutting details help too: digits like 0, 6, 8 and 9 have closed loops that need small bridges if cut from a single sheet, or they can be made as separate raised pieces mounted on stand-offs.

## What your community requires

Some villa communities set rules for the size, style or position of house numbers. Dubai also uses an official Makani number system for buildings. Check whether your developer or community requires specific numbers to be shown, and ask about size or finish rules before you order. Do this early, because sign choices are cheaper to change before they are made.

## A quick checklist

1. Decide the viewing distance, then choose the digit height.
2. Choose a simple, medium or bold font.
3. Check the contrast against the actual wall colour.
4. Place the number on a fixed surface, facing the approach.
5. Decide on night lighting.
6. Check community rules.
7. Tape a cardboard mock-up to the wall and look from the road.

## Frequently asked questions

**What size should villa house numbers be?**
A good starting point is 100 to 150 mm digits for a gate set back 12 to 18 m from where people first look. Go larger for wide streets.

**Should house numbers be lit?**
If the area is dark at night or the number is hard to find, yes. A soft halo or a small wall light is usually enough.

**What colour is best for house numbers?**
Whichever contrasts most strongly with the wall behind. Matte and brushed finishes avoid glare.
