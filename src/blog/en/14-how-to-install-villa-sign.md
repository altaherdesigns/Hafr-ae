Most villa walls in the UAE look solid from the outside. Under the render, many are built from concrete blocks with hollow cores, and that changes how a sign should be fixed. A screw and plastic plug that would hold well in solid concrete can grip only a thin outer shell of a hollow block.

This guide walks through the job in order: finding out what the wall is made of, choosing anchors, using a template, setting standoffs, leaving room for water to drain, and knowing when the job is better left to a fitter. It is general guidance for a typical plate or set of letters. Always follow the instructions that come with your sign and with the anchors you use.

## Step 1: Find out what the wall is made of

Ask the contractor, check the drawings, or look at a drill hole somewhere hidden. The common cases are:

- **Hollow concrete block, rendered.** The most common villa wall. Dubai Municipality's technical guide for concrete blocks sets the outer shell of a 200 mm hollow block at 30 mm, and only 20 mm for a 100 mm block. That thin shell is all a short fixing can grip.
- **Solid block or concrete.** Strong and easy to fix into.
- **Lightweight aerated (AAC) block.** Soft and easy to drill, but it needs anchors made for it.
- **Stone or tile cladding.** The sign must be fixed through the cladding into the wall behind it, which is a job for a fitter.

## Step 2: Choose anchors that suit the wall

![Section through a rendered hollow block wall: a threaded rod passes through a standoff and the render into the block's outer shell, inside a mesh sleeve filled with resin that spreads into the hollow core behind the shell](figures/fixing-hollow-block.svg "In a hollow block, a mesh sleeve keeps the resin around the rod and lets it spread behind the thin shell, where it locks in place.")

For hollow blocks, the usual choice is a chemical (resin) anchor with a mesh sleeve. The sleeve goes into the hole, resin is injected into it, and a threaded rod or stud is pushed in. Resin squeezes through the mesh into the hollow core behind the shell and sets, which locks the fixing in place.

For solid block and concrete, a good-quality plug and screw or a resin anchor without a sleeve will usually do. Use stainless steel fixings outdoors, so rust does not stain the wall below the sign.

## Step 3: Mark out with a full-size template

Never measure fixing holes off the sign itself.

1. Tape the full-size template to the wall.
2. Check it is level with a spirit level, and at the planned height. Most signs look best with their centre about 1.4 to 1.6 m above the ground, as our [size guide](/blog/villa-name-plate-size-guide/) explains.
3. Step back to the road and look again before you drill.
4. Mark each hole through the template, and check for hidden cables and pipes with a detector.

If you are fixing separate letters rather than a plate, the template matters even more. See [letters or a plate](/blog/stainless-steel-letters-vs-plate/) for why.

## Step 4: Drill, clean and set the anchors

- **Drill without hammer action in hollow block.** Anchor makers such as Hilti specify rotary-only drilling in hollow masonry, because hammering can break the inside of the shell.
- **Use the drill size and depth on the anchor's instructions.**
- **Clean every hole.** A typical routine from anchor makers is to blow the dust out twice, brush twice, and blow out twice more. Resin does not bond to dust.

Then insert the sleeve, inject the resin from the bottom of the hole, and push the rod in with a slight twist to the depth marked on it. Leave it alone until the resin has cured.

Heat speeds resin up a lot. For one common product, Hilti's data gives a working time of only 2 minutes and a curing time of 45 minutes when the wall is above 30 °C, and its listed installation range stops at 40 °C. A sun-facing wall in a UAE summer can be hotter than the air. Fit anchors early in the morning or in shade, and check the temperature range on the cartridge before you start.

## Step 5: Set the standoffs and fix the sign

Standoffs hold the sign away from the wall. Sign suppliers sell them in projections such as 15, 20 and 25 mm. The gap does three jobs: it gives the sign a shadow, it lets air and water pass behind it, and it keeps the back of the sign off a wall that may be slightly uneven.

- Use the same projection for every standoff, so the sign sits parallel to the wall.
- Tighten to the torque given in the anchor instructions, no more. Overtightening can crack render.
- Check level again before the final turn.

## Step 6: Let water drain and keep the wall clean

- **Do not seal all round the sign.** A sign on standoffs should be open at the bottom so water and dust can leave.
- **Seal the holes, not the sign.** A small bead of exterior sealant where each fixing enters the render keeps water out of the wall.
- **Clean off resin and dust** before it sets on the render.
- **Wipe the sign** with fresh water to remove fingerprints and drilling dust.

## When to call a fitter

Fixing a small, light plate to a rendered wall is a reasonable job for a careful homeowner. Call a fitter or an electrician when:

- The sign is lit and needs a cable connected to power. Electrical work should be done by a qualified electrician.
- The sign is large or heavy, or fixed high on the wall.
- The wall is clad in stone or tile.
- You are fixing many separate letters in a long line.
- You are not sure what the wall is made of.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use plastic wall plugs in a hollow block wall?**
For a very light sign, sometimes. For anything heavier, use resin anchors with mesh sleeves, which grip much better behind the thin shell.

**How far should a sign stand off the wall?**
Standoffs are commonly sold in projections from 15 to 25 mm. Use the length your sign maker recommends, and the same length at every point.

**Can I install a sign in summer?**
Yes, but work early in the day. Resin sets very quickly on a hot wall, and some products are not rated above 40 °C.

**Should I seal around the edge of the sign?**
No. Seal only where the fixings enter the wall, and leave the bottom open so water can drain.
