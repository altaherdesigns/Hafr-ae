The proof is the last moment a custom sign can be changed for free. Once you approve it, the metal is cut, and a mistake becomes a new sign. Yet most people glance at a proof on a phone, see their name, and reply "looks good".

This guide gives you a checklist to work through before you approve: spelling, letter shapes, size on the wall, fixing, colour and sign-off. It is the cheapest quality check you will ever make.

## What a proof should show

A proof is a drawing of exactly what will be cut. Before you check the details, make sure it shows enough to check. A useful proof includes:

- The final text, exactly as it will be cut
- The overall width and height, in millimetres
- The material, thickness and finish
- The positions of fixings, and any bridges in the cut
- A view of the sign on a photo of your wall, at the right scale
- Any lighting, and where the cable leaves the sign
- A reference number or date, so you know which version you approved

If the proof is only a picture of the name, ask for the rest before you approve anything.

## Step 1: Check the spelling, letter by letter

Read every letter, not the word. The eye fills in familiar names and skips errors.

- **Arabic:** check every dot. A single misplaced dot changes the letter, and the name. Check the hamza, any shadda or vowel marks you asked for, and how the definite article al- joins the name.
- **English:** check the transliteration against the spelling you use on passports and documents. Al and El are both common, but there should be one spelling, used everywhere.
- **Numbers:** check the villa or plot number against your title deed or utility bill, not from memory.

Then ask a second person who reads the script well to check it independently. Do this even if you are a native reader. You are checking for what you expect to see; they will read what is there.

## Step 2: Check the shapes, not just the words

In cut metal, a letter is a physical shape. Look at the drawing large, zoomed in, and check:

- **Joins.** Arabic letters that should connect are connected, and letters that should be separate are not touching.
- **Dots and small marks.** Each one is either cut out, raised, or held by a small bridge, and none will simply fall out.
- **Bridges.** Closed shapes such as the loops in waw or meem need small connections to hold their centres in place. The bridges should look deliberate, not like accidents. The [calligraphy guide](/blog/arabic-calligraphy-styles-family-name-sign/) shows why they are needed.
- **Thin strokes.** Very fine lines may need thickening to survive cutting and weather. Ask if any were changed.

## Step 3: Check the size on your wall

A proof seen on a phone is always small. Check it at real size.

1. Ask for the proof placed on a photo of your wall, scaled from a measurement.
2. Print it at full size, or draw the outline on cardboard, and tape it up at the planned height.
3. Look from the road, from the gate, and at dusk.

If anything feels too small or too large, change it now. Our [size guide](/blog/villa-name-plate-size-guide/) explains how big a sign should be against its wall.

## Step 4: Check fixings, lighting and the back

- **Fixings.** Are they where the wall is solid, and away from joints, pipes and cables?
- **Standoffs.** How far will the sign sit from the wall?
- **Lighting.** Where does the cable leave the sign, and is there power at that point?
- **Weight.** Large plates are heavy. Confirm the fixings suit your wall.

If you are choosing between a plate and separate letters, [letters or a plate](/blog/stainless-steel-letters-vs-plate/) explains how each is fixed.

## Step 5: Check colour and finish against a reference

Screens do not show colour accurately. Agree the colour by a reference rather than by eye.

- For powder coat, use a RAL code, such as RAL 9005 for jet black.
- For stainless steel, name the finish: brushed or polished, and the direction of the brush lines.
- If the sign must match a gate or door, use the same RAL code for both.

## Step 6: Sign off in writing

Approve the proof in writing, on the final version, by reply on the same message thread or by signing the drawing. Keep a copy. A written sign-off protects you as well as the maker: if the sign arrives different from the proof, you have the drawing to compare it against.

### The proof checklist

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Every Arabic letter and dot | One dot changes the name |
| English spelling against documents | One spelling, used everywhere |
| Number against a document | Memory is not a source |
| A second reader | They read what is there |
| Joins, dots and bridges | Nothing falls out of the cut |
| Full-size mock-up on the wall | Phones make everything look small |
| Fixings and cable position | The wall must be solid there |
| Colour by RAL code or finish name | Screens are not accurate |
| Written approval of the final version | Something to compare against |

## Frequently asked questions

**How many changes can I make to a proof?**
Ask before you order. Small corrections are normal; a change of layout may be treated as a new design.

**Do I need to check a proof if I am a native reader?**
Yes. Ask someone else to read it too. Native readers are often the quickest to skip over a familiar word.

**Can I approve a proof on my phone?**
You can reply from your phone, but check the drawing on a larger screen or a printout first.
